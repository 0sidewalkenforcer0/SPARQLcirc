# Paper figure design and captions

This file records the design rationale for the paper figures generated by
`make_figures.py`.  The numerical source of every panel is a committed CSV under
`reference/`; the plotting script contains no experimental result constants.

## What the closest systems do well

### SPARQLprov

The [SPARQLprov paper](https://vldb.org/pvldb/vol14/p3389-galarraga.pdf) organizes
its evaluation around controlled overhead:

- Figure 2 groups query templates and repeats the same comparison over three
  reification schemes.  Color distinguishes engine and execution mode; a log
  axis and a dotted timeout line keep failures visible.
- Figure 3 uses small multiples for TPC-H query templates.  Scale factor is the
  ordered independent variable, engine is encoded by color, and query mode is
  encoded by line style.
- Figure 4 decomposes elapsed time into baseline, reification, and provenance
  overhead, so the paper can explain *why* the total changes.

The useful lesson is to separate the source of overhead from the total.  The
main weakness to avoid is density: its template-level figures carry many series
and require a large legend.

### NPCS

The [NPCS paper](https://doi.org/10.1145/3589334.3645557) makes the competitor
comparison the visual center of the evaluation:

- Figures 2 and 4 show plain SPARQL, NPCS, and SPARQLprov for every WatDiv query
  class, repeated across engines, data sizes, and reification schemes.
- Timeout is not silently dropped: it is shown at the upper boundary of a
  broken/logarithmic axis.
- Figure 5 changes the independent variable for the real workload: queries are
  ordered by answer count and execution time is plotted on a log scale, once per
  engine.

The useful lesson is that a system comparison should preserve failures and the
workload dimension.  We avoid NPCS's very dense query labels by using smaller,
claim-specific panels.

### ProvSQL

The latest [ProvSQL system paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.12058) uses a sequence
of diagnostic figures rather than a single headline chart:

- Figure 1 plots execution time and provenance overhead across scale factors,
  with small multiples for individual queries.
- Figures 2--4 separate circuit construction, semiring evaluation, probability
  evaluation, implementation variants, and gate count.
- Figures 5--7 use matched queries for direct system comparisons and retain OOM
  or timeout outcomes in the surrounding support matrix.

The useful lesson is causal pairing: runtime should be shown beside the circuit
or stage that explains it.  We avoid dual y-axes because they make magnitude
comparisons harder.

## Visual rules used here

- Double-column width: 7.15 inches; 7--8 pt text at final size.
- Vector PDF is the primary artifact; a 300-dpi PNG is generated for previews.
- Okabe--Ito colors plus redundant markers, line styles, and open/filled symbols.
- Lines only connect an ordered numeric variable.  Direct categorical comparisons
  use the compact grouped columns and hatching familiar from SPARQLprov/NPCS.
- Log scales are used for wide ranges.  Log-column panels start at the explicitly
  visible 1 ms or 1 KiB floor; their endpoints encode the observations and their
  areas must not be interpreted linearly.
- Panel labels are lowercase (`a`, `b`, `c`); long conclusions stay in captions.
- Timeouts and failures remain visible as boundary markers or explicit counts.
- Error bars are shown only where repeated runs exist and are defined as the
  standard deviation across the five timed runs.
- Method colors are stable: blue is SPARQLcirc/shared, white with diagonal hatch
  is the plain or competing method, and orange denotes compilation/per-answer
  completion where the comparison is not a two-system bar chart.

## Paper figures

### Figure 1 — knowledge compilation

Files: `figures/paper_fig1_compilation.pdf` and `.png`.

**Caption.** Compiled representation size. (a) At fixed treewidth two, the
fixed-order OBDD grows from 4 to 299,481 nodes and then reaches the 120 s timeout,
whereas d4 produces d-DNNFs of at most 5,270 nodes. Downward triangles denote
timeouts, not measured OBDD sizes. (b) With fixed depth and increasing
treewidth, both representations grow rapidly; d-DNNF becomes smaller from
treewidth five. Values are from E4.

### Figure 2 — benefit and boundary of sharing

Files: `figures/paper_fig2_sharing.pdf` and `.png`.

**Caption.** When a shared circuit helps, and when it does not. (a) The
structural ratio between a per-answer representation and the shared circuit
grows with reconvergence, reaching 201× on the deep synthetic family.
(b) Completing a per-answer NPCS/SPARQLprov-style representation with the same
compiler repeats compilation work; one shared pass is 8.7× faster at 1,000
answers. (c--d) On the three measured low-sharing queries, the committed
clean-room NPCS reimplementation is as fast or faster and emits 12.4--13.4×
fewer serialized bytes than SPARQLcirc. Blue labels report SPARQLcirc/NPCS.
These two panels measure
construction and UTF-8 result volume only: NPCS stops at how-provenance and does
not perform PQE. Values are from E2, E11, and G2b.

### Figure 3 — construction on stock engines

Files: `figures/paper_fig3_construction.pdf` and `.png`.

**Caption.** Circuit construction on an unmodified SPARQL engine. (a--b)
Execution times for the clean-room NPCS-compatible per-answer provenance SELECT and the
circuit-producing CONSTRUCT on WatDiv 10M and 100M. S, F, L, and M denote star,
snowflake, linear-path, and MINUS templates; labels give CONSTRUCT/NPCS. S/F/L
come from E3 and M from E6. The legacy CSV column is named `plain_ms`, but its
harness calls `get_npcs()`; it is neither the original-query baseline nor an
official-NPCS artifact measurement. The
logarithmic columns start at 1 ms. (c)
Construction time versus derivation count for the 31 completed Wikidata single
queries on the 2.13B-triple corpus. The log plot omits completed queries with
zero derivations; the annotation reports all 41 outcomes. These are single-run
reach/scale measurements, not a repeated microbenchmark.

### Figure 4 — exact PQE and its cost

Files: `figures/paper_fig4_pqe.pdf` and `.png`.

**Caption.** Exact probabilistic query evaluation. (a) End-to-end latency of
SPARQLcirc and ProvSQL on five TPC-H Q3 market segments. Bars are five-run
medians and error bars are run standard deviations. Probability equality is
validated separately by R8.3 (`max_abs_error = 0`); this panel compares latency.
(b) SPARQLprov-style decomposition of median end-to-end time into construction,
compilation, and WMC. Bar length is the fraction of the stage sum, while the
right-hand label gives the absolute median total. WMC accounts for at most 0.6%
on all three workloads. Values are from G4 and G3.

### Table 1 — independent evaluation routes

Files: `figures/paper_table1_validation.pdf` and `.png`.

**Caption.** Maximum absolute probability error on 26 sampled answer circuits.
OBDD and d4 evaluate the same emitted circuit and both agree exactly with
possible-world enumeration. The sample contains all 16 checked property-path
answers but only eight TPC-H Q3 answers. Values are from G6.
